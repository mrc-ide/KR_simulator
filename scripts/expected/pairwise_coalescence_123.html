Pick two current-generation parasite strains. Each strain's full ancestry back to the founders is traced and drawn in its own color; where those two ancestries overlap is a shared ancestor, and the most recent shared ancestor(s) are the pedigree's coalescence point(s) for this pair.

Reading this correctly: this is the transmission pedigree — it shows when the two strains' host lineages last shared a parasite, generation by generation, regardless of genome position. A shared ancestor here does not guarantee any specific genomic region was actually inherited from it — recombination can replace a segment with DNA from the other parent at every step. For the true, recombination-aware, per-genomic-interval coalescence time, use coal_T_{seed}.csv / pedigree_relate_strain_genome_{seed}.csv instead. Also note a strain can have up to two immediate parents (one per gamete in the oocyst) — so the ancestor count can up to double each generation back, fewer if the same strain is drawn twice (a clonal self-cross) or if lineages merge.
